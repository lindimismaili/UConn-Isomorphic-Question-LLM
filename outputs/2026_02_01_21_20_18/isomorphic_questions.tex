\QBlock{Q1}{An object starts with an initial velocity of $4.5\,\text{m/s}$ and moves along a straight line with a constant acceleration of $-0.9\,\text{m/s}^2$. What is the displacement of the object when its velocity is $-3.0\,\text{m/s}$?}{
  \Correct{-40.5 m}{To find the displacement, use the kinematic equation $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$, where $v$ is the final velocity, $u$ is the initial velocity, $a$ is the acceleration, and $s$ is the displacement. \n Here, $v = -3.0\,\text{m/s}$, $u = 4.5\,\text{m/s}$, and $a = -0.9\,\text{m/s}^2$.\n Solving for $s$, we have $(-3.0)^2 = (4.5)^2 + 2*(-0.9)*s$.\n This simplifies to $9 = 20.25 - 1.8s$, leading to $1.8s = 11.25$, thus $s = -40.5\,\text{m}$.\n The negative sign indicates displacement in the opposite direction of the initial velocity.}
  \Wrong{-30.5 m}{This choice results from wrongly adding the squares of the velocities, as in $(v+u)^2=0.5a^2s$.}
  \Wrong{-20.0 m}{This choice uses half the acceleration value, a common error thinking initial acceleration is different from constant acceleration.}
  \Wrong{-10.5 m}{This choice arises from incorrectly applying the equation $v = u + at$ instead of using the kinematic equation involving displacement.}
}
