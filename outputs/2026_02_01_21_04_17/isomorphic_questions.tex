\QBlock{Q1}{A dolphin leaps out of the water with a speed of 12 m/s at an angle $\phi$. What is the speed of the dolphin when it is at its maximum height?}{
  \Wrong{12 m/s.}{At maximum height, the vertical component of velocity is zero, while the horizontal component remains constant.}
  \Correct{12 \cos \phi\, \text{m/s}.}{Correct. The horizontal component of the velocity, $v_{x} = v \cos \phi$, remains the same throughout the motion.}
  \Wrong{12 \sin \phi\, \text{m/s}.}{This represents the initial vertical component, which becomes zero at maximum height.}
  \Wrong{0 m/s.}{The horizontal component of velocity is not zero at the maximum height.}
}

\QBlock{Q2}{A rover is moving on a flat surface with an initial velocity of $\mathbf{v} = (15.5\, \text{m/s}) \hat{i}$ when its thrusters produce an acceleration of $\mathbf{a} = (3.0\, \text{m/s}^2) \hat{i} + (1.5\, \text{m/s}^2) \hat{j}$. Based on this scenario, which statement is true?}{
  \Wrong{The x-component of the rover’s velocity stays the same.}{The x-velocity changes due to the i-direction acceleration.}
  \Wrong{The y-component of the rover’s velocity stays the same.}{The y-velocity changes due to the j-direction acceleration.}
  \Correct{Both the speed and direction change.}{Correct. Both the x and y components of velocity change, affecting the speed and direction.}
  \Wrong{None of the statements above are correct.}{This is incorrect as option C accurately describes the situation.}
}
