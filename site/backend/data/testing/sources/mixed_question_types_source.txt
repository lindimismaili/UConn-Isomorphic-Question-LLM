1. During a week-long field test for an autonomous hospital delivery cart, engineers notice that the cart enters the final straight section of the corridor at \(4.8\,\text{m/s}\) while a constant braking system can provide only \(1.6\,\text{m/s}^2\) of deceleration. The cart is \(6.5\,\text{m}\) from a sealed lab door, and the faculty supervisor wants a written justification rather than a single numerical answer. Explain whether the cart stops before it reaches the door, and show how the relevant kinematics relationship supports your conclusion.

2. A biomedical sensor package is launched straight upward from the roof of a clinic with an initial speed of \(18\,\text{m/s}\). Ignore air resistance unless a part states otherwise.
(a) Determine the time it takes the package to reach its maximum height.
(b) Which statement best describes the package's acceleration while it is in flight?
A. The acceleration is zero at the top of the path.
B. The acceleration is downward and approximately \(9.8\,\text{m/s}^2\) throughout the flight.
C. The acceleration points upward while the package rises and downward while it falls.
D. The acceleration decreases linearly to zero as the package approaches the top.

3. Figure 1 shows the velocity-time graph of a prototype ambulance during a braking test. Which statement is correct?
A. The slope of the graph is the vehicle's displacement.
B. The area under the graph is the vehicle's acceleration.
C. The slope of the graph gives the vehicle's acceleration.
D. The graph proves that the braking force is zero.
